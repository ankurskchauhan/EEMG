Extreme Environments Materials Group

Department of Materials Engineering, Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru-560012, India

The Extreme Environments Materials Group (EEMG), led by Ankur Chauhan, is affiliated with IISc, Bengaluru. Our group focuses on understanding materials behavior in extreme environments relevant to energy, space, & defense applications.

We have been supported and funded by ISRO, SERB and the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF), among other agencies.

Interested in joining? See Openings.

Research areas

Energy & space

Fatigue, creep and high-temperature response of steels and novel alloys.

Nuclear

Irradiation effects in tungsten, steels and multi-principal element alloys.

Defense

Shock, impact and ceramics such as boron carbide.

November 9, 2026

IUMRS-ICA 2026: Outstanding Poster Presentation Award. A proud moment for our group! Golu Gehlot received the Outstanding Poster Presentation Award at the International Union of Materials Research Societies conference (IUMRS-ICA 2026), held in Tainan, Taiwan, from October 31 to November 4, 2026. The award recognised our work entitled “Tailoring Strength–Ductility Balance in a novel Al–Mg–Mn–Sc–Zr Alloy through Multi-Route Rolling”, which explores how different rolling routes can be used to tailor microstructure and improve the strength–ductility balance.

October 17, 2026

New paper in International Journal of Fatigue. Bright's work on “Microstructural origins of fatigue crack growth anisotropy in thermomechanically rolled ultrafine bainitic and martensitic steels” is now published. The study shows how prior-austenite grain boundary orientation and martensite–austenite constituent distribution influence crack propagation: boundaries perpendicular to the crack path deflect it and retard growth, while parallel ones facilitate intergranular fracture. Retained-austenite transformation and crack closure further improve resistance, particularly in UFB steel. Done in collaboration with the Physical Metallurgy group led by Prof. Mahesh Somani, University of Oulu, Finland.

October 8, 2026

New paper in Materials Science and Engineering A. Abhinav's work on “Plasticity and damage initiation in textured Zr–2.5Nb pressure tube material: A slip trace analysis-based study” is published. Combining slip-trace analysis of nearly 200 α grains with EBSD, TEM and resolved shear stress analysis, the study shows how crystallographic texture governs deformation mechanisms along the longitudinal and circumferential directions, and how limited slip transfer across α–β interfaces promotes interfacial cavitation and damage initiation. Done with researchers at the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC).

July 21, 2026

New paper in Acta Materialia. Shubham's “Solute-defect interaction and oxidation govern high-temperature low-cycle fatigue in Mo-added CoCrNi-based multi-principal element alloy”. Tensile and low-cycle fatigue testing at room temperature and 550 °C, combined with electron microscopy and atom probe tomography, shows that Suzuki-type segregation at stacking faults promotes dynamic strain ageing, planar slip and twinning, giving continuous cyclic hardening and reduced fatigue life, while grain-boundary segregation and oxidation shift crack initiation to grain boundaries. Done with Prof. Surendra Makenini's group at IISc and Dr Govind Bajargan at VSSC, ISRO.

May 30, 2026

New paper in Advanced Engineering Materials. Aparajita's “Creep-Induced Microstructural Evolution in an A2–B2 Superalloy”, a collaboration with Ruhr University Bochum and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT). Creep testing at 1030 °C reveals a stress-dependent transition in deformation mechanisms driven by dislocation–precipitate interactions, with precipitate morphology and A2/B2 interfacial coherency playing critical roles.
